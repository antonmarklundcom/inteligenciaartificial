## 1. Revenue lines

### 1.2 Talleres / capacitación

### 1.3 Sprints de Implementación — **the volume line**

| Tier | Scope | Price | Days | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Asistente de conocimiento interno / documentos | USD 3,500 · Gs. 25.500.000 | 8–10 | Most repeatable |
| B | Automatización de flujo (intake, clasificación, generación) | USD 5,500 · Gs. 40.000.000 | 10–14 | Integration risk |

Plus client-paid recurring costs (API, hosting, licences), quoted explicitly and
separately. Floor USD 3,000.
**Margin 50–65%**, degrading fast with scope drift. **Track actual vs quoted days on
every project — this is the single most important internal number in the business.**
**Validation:** first Sprint sold from a delivered audit; first Sprint delivered within
120% of quoted days before selling a third.
**Sequencing:** Phase 0–1 for the first, then continuously, capped at 2 concurrent.

### 1.4 Chatbot / asistente de atención
Not a separate line — it is **Sprint Tier C**. Kept distinct in marketing (it is what
people search for) but identical in contracting and delivery. Selling it as its own
category invites the "cheap chatbot" price comparison we are trying to avoid.

### 1.5 Retainers de Optimización — **the durability line**

| Tier | Price/month | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Base | USD 800 · Gs. 5.800.000 | Monitoring, 4h changes, monthly report |
| Activo | USD 1,500 · Gs. 11.000.000 | 10h changes, quarterly opportunity review, priority support |
| Socio | USD 2,500 · Gs. 18.000.000 | 18h, dedicated roadmap, quarterly on-site |

**Margin 60–75%.** Minimum 3-month term. Hours roll over one month maximum.
**Never sold cold** — only after a delivered Sprint.
**Validation:** 50% attach rate on the first four Sprints.
**Strategic role:** this is what converts a project business into a company with
predictable revenue. Four Activo retainers ≈ USD 6,000/month of base load, which is
what makes the capacity ceiling survivable.
**Risk:** perceived value decay. The monthly report **is** the product — if it is
skipped, the retainer churns.

### 1.6 Plantillas y guías (productos digitales)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | USD 25–90 · Gs. 180.000–650.000 |
| **Products** | Vertical prompt packs · policy template with guidance · AI vendor evaluation kit · process measurement toolkit |
| **Fulfilment** | Zero marginal |
| **Scalability** | High per unit, **low in absolute revenue** — a realistic ceiling here is a few hundred USD/month in a market this size |
| **Real value** | Not revenue. It is (a) a low-friction first transaction that starts a commercial relationship, and (b) an upsell path from the free prompt library |
| **Validation** | Publish one paid pack in Phase 5 and measure. If it earns less than one workshop per quarter, stop. |
| **Sequencing** | **Phase 5. Not before.** Every hour spent productising before delivery is proven is an hour not spent proving delivery. |
| **Honest constraint** | Payments in Paraguay add a provider, tax handling and invoicing obligations for a small revenue line. That overhead is the real reason to defer, not the build cost. |

### 1.7 Cursos
Self-paced course, USD 90–250. **Defer past Phase 5 / probably never.** Course
production is a different business with different economics (marketing-heavy, high
production cost, low completion rates), and it competes for the same hours as
delivery. The workshop already monetises the same knowledge at a far better hourly
rate. Revisit only if workshop demand exceeds delivery capacity by a wide margin.

### 1.8 Suscripciones a herramientas — **rejected for Phase 1–4**
A paid tier on the assessment or calculator would require accounts, payments,
subscription management, invoicing, dunning and support, to monetise tools whose
strategic job is **lead generation**. Charging for them reduces the funnel that feeds
a business with 60–75% margins. `subscription_plans` stays unbuilt (`08` §7) until
there is a tool with proven standalone demand and a buyer distinct from our consulting
buyer. **Current evidence: none. Decision: do not build.**

### 1.9 Directorio patrocinado — **rejected**
Sponsored placements would compromise the vendor-neutral advisory position that is our
main differentiator, for a revenue line worth less than one workshop. Not worth it at
any price. (`04` §4 rejects the directory itself for independent reasons.)

### 1.10 Referral partnerships

| Direction | Structure | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Inbound** (contadores, agencias refer to us) | 10% of first project value, paid on collection, disclosed to the client | Highest-quality lead source in this market |
| **Outbound** (we refer out work we decline) | No commission taken | Goodwill; keeps `/que-no-hacemos` credible; the referral comes back |
| **Vendor partnerships** (CRM/ERP vendors incl. VenderCRM) | Mutual referral, we are the implementation partner | Warm, pre-qualified pipeline |

Never accept a commission for recommending a tool to a client without disclosing it.
Undisclosed vendor commissions destroy advisory credibility permanently, and in a
market this small the client will eventually find out.

## 2. Revenue mix and sequencing

| | Months 1–6 | Months 7–12 | Months 13–18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Auditorías | 45% | 25% | 20% |
| Sprints | 30% | 40% | 40% |
| Talleres | 25% | 15% | 10% |
| Retainers | 0% | 20% | 25% |
| Productos | 0% | 0% | 5% |

**Targets, not forecasts [SUPUESTO].** Their purpose is to force sequencing: audits
and workshops fund the early months because they are sellable before we have a track
record; Sprints take over as delivery proof accumulates; retainers provide the base
load that makes the capacity ceiling survivable.

**Capacity-derived ceiling:** roughly USD 14–18k/month at full utilisation with one
principal and one fractional implementer (`02` §8). This is a *ceiling*, not a target.
Growing past it requires hiring, which requires standardisation to be finished first —
which is why the standardised-vs-custom ratio (`02` §9) is a quarterly review item.

## 3. Validation gates

No line advances without passing its gate:

| Gate | Test | If failed |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | 3 audits sold within 8 weeks of Phase 0 outreach | Revisit ICP, offer framing, or price before building anything |
| **G2** | First audit delivered in ≤ 6.5 person-days | The audit template is not standardised enough; fix before selling more |
| **G3** | ≥ 1 of the first 3 audits converts to a Sprint | The audit is not producing actionable findings; rework the methodology |
| **G4** | First Sprint delivered within 120% of quoted days | Re-price the tier or narrow its scope |
| **G5** | ≥ 2 of the first 4 Sprints attach a retainer | The retainer's value story is not landing; rework the monthly report |
| **G6** | Phase 2 tool produces ≥ 5 band-A/B leads in 90 days | The funnel is not the problem to invest in next; redirect to referral and outbound |
| **G7** | Standardised/configured delivery hours ≥ 60% by month 12 | The business is not scalable; stop taking custom work |

**G1 is the hard one.** It happens before any application code exists, and failing it
means the positioning is wrong — which is far cheaper to learn in week 8 than in
month 8.

## 4. Pricing rules

1. **Never discount below the floor.** Reduce scope instead, or decline.
2. **Fixed price, always.** Hourly billing transfers estimation risk to the client and destroys the differentiator.
3. **Change orders are priced at the day rate** and require written approval before work.
4. **Third-party recurring costs are always the client's**, quoted as a range before signature, never absorbed silently into our price.
5. **Prices published on the site** as "desde", with the scope that price buys.
6. **Payment before work.** Audit 100% up front; Sprint 40/40/20; retainer monthly in advance.
7. **Re-price when actual days exceed quoted days on two consecutive projects in a tier.** The data, not the sentiment, triggers the change.
8. **Annual review**, plus an FX review each quarter.
